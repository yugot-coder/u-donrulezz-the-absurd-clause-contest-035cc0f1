# The Absurd Clause Contest

> By clicking Accept, you consent to us naming one (1) company pigeon after you, and to that pigeon judging you silently from the roof.

This clause wins on the judge's own terms: it opens in pure boilerplate
("By clicking Accept, you consent to us naming one (1) company pigeon") —
the numbered-quantity legalism is exactly how real ToS sentences sound —
then ambushes the reader with a concrete absurd image, a pigeon "judging
you silently from the roof", that lands on one reading with no setup and no
explaining of its own joke. It avoids the genre's tired clichés (souls,
firstborns, kidneys, arm-wrestling CEOs) and stays tight at 133 characters,
one clean sentence. Ten candidates across different angles were drafted in
`CANDIDATES.md`; this one was picked for the strongest deadpan-to-absurd
turn and the most specific image.

## How it runs

`main.py` is stdlib only and deterministic — no network, no randomness, no
clock. It asserts the kit gate (1–200 characters, one line, one sentence)
and writes the clause to `entry.txt` under `MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR`
(default `/output`), then reads the file back and verifies it byte-for-byte.

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=/tmp/out python3 main.py
cat /tmp/out/entry.txt
```
